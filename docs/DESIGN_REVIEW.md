## Iteration 1 — original design brief

Initially reviewed 2026-11-04 against `/home/parth/Desktop/Python/phone-gpt/docs/PRODUCT_REQUIREMENTS.md`, before implementation or screenshot inspection. These are research-informed design judgments, not findings from PhoneGPT user testing.

## Direction and hierarchy

Lead with a plain explanation of the product, show a concrete example, and offer one clear next action. This applies [NNgroup’s homepage principles](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/homepage-design-principles/): communicate purpose, reveal content through examples, and make navigation/action clear. The sequence below is a PhoneGPT-specific recommendation.

1. **Header:** supplied logo and PhoneGPT wordmark at left; Features, How it works, FAQ, and Contact links at right, followed by the primary CTA. Logo links home; section links use descriptive anchor destinations. Keep founder biography out of the header.
2. **Hero — what it is:** eyebrow “On-device AI for Android”; H1 “An AI assistant that runs on your phone.” Supporting copy: “Write, brainstorm, and ask questions with a model you choose. Download your models first, then chat and dictate messages locally—without a PhoneGPT account.” Main CTA below copy; secondary text link “See how it works.” Small setup note: “Internet required for app and model downloads. Device memory and model compatibility vary.” Place a rounded phone illustration beside the copy, with a visible concept label.
3. **Why local — why it matters:** a broad editorial band, headed “Keep everyday conversations on your device.” Explain local inference and conversation storage, model choice, and use without reliable connectivity after setup. Use three short statements separated by rules rather than another card grid. Link to a factual privacy explanation; avoid encryption or absolute device-backup claims.
4. **How it works:** three numbered steps: install the Android app; download and load a compatible chat model; start chatting, optionally downloading an English voice model. Use a horizontal step sequence on desktop and a vertical sequence on mobile. Put storage/RAM variability beneath the model step. Downloaded and loaded are distinct states.
5. **Features — show the experience:** alternate an illustration/detail panel and explanatory text across two spacious rows. First: model choice plus local chat/history/search. Second: English voice typing into an editable draft, plus questions about UTF-8 `.txt` files up to 32 KB. Use short subheads and examples, not a wall of capability badges. Explain that voice typing does not automatically send messages or speak replies.
6. **Use cases and audience:** “For the things you do every day.” Three concrete examples: draft a personal note; brainstorm while travelling after setup; ask questions about a short text file. Follow with a sentence for people who want to experiment with downloadable models. Avoid workplace compliance claims or unverified performance promises.
7. **Availability and FAQ:** Android build availability, setup/download needs, phone memory, supported files, voice language, and privacy limits. Say “iOS is in development; no verified iPhone release yet.” Keep availability visible above any collapsed FAQ answers. Do not use active store badges without a verified listing.
8. **Final founder section:** “Help shape PhoneGPT” with a short creator credit, then one form for messages and model requests. This is the final substantive section, after visitors understand the product. End with a quiet footer containing privacy, contact, and only verified project links.

## CTA and founder form

- Use “Get PhoneGPT for Android” only when it leads to a working, verified distribution destination. If an APK is offered, label it “Download Android APK” and disclose version, file size, installation route, and relevant compatibility information at the download destination. A built APK does not establish store readiness. Until public distribution exists, use “See Android availability” linking to the availability section. Do not invent a waitlist.
- Repeat the same primary action in the header, hero, and availability section; use one visual treatment. Keep Contact secondary. The end form has its own action, clearly separate from product acquisition.
- Desktop founder layout: a short introduction at left, form at right. Mobile: introduction above form. Suggested fields: Topic (“Message” / “Model request”), Name, Reply email, Message; show required/optional status explicitly. Model request helper: “Include the model name or link and what you want to use it for.” Do not add a second competing form.
- For an email handoff, button copy is “Open email draft.” Explain before activation: “Opens your email app addressed to Parth. Review and send there.” Show a copyable the configured founder inbox fallback and preserve entries if handoff fails. Do not display “Message sent” or claim delivery. A server-backed form would need its actual handling and privacy behavior established first.
- Put visible labels above fields; placeholders can give examples but cannot replace labels. Associate help/error text with its control. These choices follow [W3C WAI form labeling guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/labels/). Show actionable inline errors and a linked error summary; announce status updates without erasing input, following [WAI notification guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/tutorials/forms/notifications/).

## Visual language and icon placement

- Start with mist `#F2F6F3`, off-white `#FAF9F5`, forest ink `#123A31`, teal `#176B60`, and coral `#D7846C`. These are proposed tokens, not verified contrast pairs. Reserve coral for tiny decorative marks; use tested forest/teal combinations for controls and readable text.
- Use a large editorial sans headline with natural line breaks, not forced word-by-word stacking. Suggested starting range: 56–76 px desktop, 36–44 px mobile; body 17–18 px, line height around 1.6, readable text width about 60–70 characters. Let headings scale rather than clip.
- Desktop hero: roughly 55% copy / 45% illustration inside a centered 1120–1200 px content width. Soft phone corners, restrained shadow, and subtle layered surfaces; avoid an oversized device drowning out the explanation. Alternate broad bands, steps, and feature rows to create rhythm.
- Put a small consistent line icon before each feature subheading: phone for local processing, layers for model choice, microphone for voice typing, file-text for TXT questions. Keep icons subordinate to the words. Use a chevron after expandable FAQ labels and a download icon before an actual download action. Decorative icons are hidden from assistive technology; icon-only controls need accessible names. Avoid padlock imagery that could suggest verified encryption.

## Mobile and accessibility acceptance

- Below the layout breakpoint, use one column in reading order: hero copy, actions, setup note, illustration. Start with 20 px side gutters; allow 16 px at the narrowest widths. Stack actions when labels no longer fit. Feature rows and founder form follow document order without CSS reordering.
- Use a labeled Menu button when navigation cannot fit. Expose expanded state, support keyboard operation and Escape, and return focus when closed. Avoid fixed bottom CTAs over the form or keyboard. Sticky headers must not hide focused controls or anchor headings.
- Apply [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/): normal text contrast ≥4.5:1; large text ≥3:1; necessary control boundaries and graphical information ≥3:1. Do not communicate state through color alone. Provide keyboard access, visible focus, semantic landmarks/headings, a skip link, and meaningful image alternatives.
- Verify reflow at 320 CSS px and 400% zoom, plus text resizing to 200%. Prevent horizontal page scrolling and clipped fields. WCAG AA target minimum is 24×24 CSS px subject to exceptions; prefer 44×44 px for this site’s buttons and menu controls. Keep focus unobscured.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences; avoid autoplaying decorative movement. Demonstrations must remain understandable without animation. Use labeled, accessible native form controls and appropriate email autocomplete/input behavior.

## Illustration, demo, and screenshot labels

- Place “Illustrative interface · not an app screenshot” directly beneath the hero phone, visible at every breakpoint. Include it in the figure caption rather than a tooltip. Example conversation text must be clearly marked as illustrative; it is not a measured output or product guarantee.
- Keep decorative mock controls noninteractive and out of the tab order. If a demonstration is interactive, label it “Website demo · sample content” and explain its behavior; do not imply it runs the actual local model.
- Future real captures should identify platform, version, and capture context, for example “Android app · v[verified version] · [device].” Identify edited/composited images and sample conversations. Caption model/device-dependent results accordingly. Label videos as recordings rather than live demos and provide captions/transcript when needed.
- No invented ratings, download counts, testimonials, customer logos, benchmark speeds, or “most popular” model badges. “Preferred” is an editorial recommendation, not usage evidence. Do not illustrate PDFs/images, spoken replies, cloud backup, or shipped iOS as current capabilities.
